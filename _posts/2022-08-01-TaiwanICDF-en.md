<p>This post was updated on August 1, 2022.</p>
<p>The <a href="https://www.icdf.org.tw/wSite/np?ctNode=31561">ICDF Scholarship</a> is backed by the Taiwan International Cooperation and Development Fund. Coincidentally, the ICDF actually started from an agricultural support project from Taiwan to Vietnam way back in 1959. Now, the fund has become a key part of maintaining relationships and promoting Taiwan's image to its allies and the region. This includes investing in human resources, economic development, and fighting climate change and poverty. A portion of the fund is used to grant annual scholarships for ICDF's own programs.</p>
<p>Speaking of the study programs and the scholarship, if you're familiar with Erasmus Mundus, you'll see that the ICDF is organized very similarly. ICDF grants scholarships for specific bachelor's, master's, and PhD programs in Taiwan, with a special focus on economic development, environment, agriculture, and public health (<a href="https://www.icdf.org.tw/wSite/DownloadFile?type=attach&file=f1646496795738.pdf&realname=Study+Programs+for+2022+Scholarship+Application.pdf">2022 Study Programs</a>). These are all Master by coursework programs, meaning you just attend classes and write a final thesis without a heavy emphasis on scientific research, especially for Master of Arts students. That said, you still need to find a supervisor and do your research early on, it won't hurt, especially for you Master of Science folks. The scholarship provides a monthly stipend of NT$12,000 for undergrads, NT$15,000 for master's students, and NT$17,000 for PhDs. You also get your tuition, airfare, books, and dorm fees waived. Personally, I think this amount is just right for bachelor's and master's students.</p>
<p>Plus, studying with an ICDF scholarship gives you a lot of independence. Since your money comes from the fund, not from your advisor or the university, you have a lot of freedom. No need to join any of your professor's projects, no chasing deadlines or worrying that a delayed project will affect your graduation. You'll graduate automatically, folks =)) Your classmates will also be mostly international students (from Asia, Latin America, and various island nations), letting you experience a multicultural environment. Like in my class, there were five of us, each from a different country.</p>

<p>The application checklist for the ICDF includes:</p>
<p>- A scanned copy of your passport<br>- Transcripts, diploma (in English).<br>- Language certificate (IELTS/TOEFL/TOEIC/or a direct interview).<br>- 2 letters of recommendation.<br>- The ICDF scholarship application form.<br>- Other documents (study plan, research proposal, CV, etc.).</p>
<p>Check out the other requirements here: <a href="https://www.icdf.org.tw/wSite/ct?xItem=12506&ctNode=31563&mp=2#aC">Eligibility</a></p>
---
<p>The selection process has 3 main steps: <a href="https://www.icdf.org.tw/wSite/DownloadFile?type=attach&file=1122812215166.PDF&realname=1122812215166.PDF">Chart of Application Procedure</a><br> - Step 1: Applicants submit their admission application to the university; then, register on the ICDF system to get the application form; Print this form and send it with other documents to the Taipei Economic and Cultural Office in Hanoi/Ho Chi Minh City. <br>- Step 2: The Embassy and the university will independently select and nominate suitable candidates to the fund.<br>- Step 3: The university and the ICDF will meet and decide on the final list of awardees.<br> So, just because you get an admission letter from the university in June doesn't mean you're guaranteed to get the scholarship from the fund.</p>
<p>Typically, the ICDF scholarship application opens annually from January to mid-March. If you're planning to give it a shot, you should set aside about 4 to 6 months before the deadline to prepare your documents—the sooner, the better. For tips on preparing your application, you can check out my other posts and advice from those who've gone before. Personally, I think you need to spend at least 2 to 3 months perfecting your study plan—this is the most critical requirement! If needed, you should also take 1-2 months to build a solid research proposal, especially for those applying for a Master of Science.</p>
